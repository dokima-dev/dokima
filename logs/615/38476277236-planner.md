> 
> Before an agent files an issue from something it raised, it must decide what the raise is, so we stop filing symptoms one by one (whack-a-mole) and bloating the backlog.
> 
> Its hand-back records these decisions, and code rejects a filing without them:
> 
> 1. **Searched:** the open issues it checked for the same cause, listed.
> 2. **Verdict**, one of:
>    - **Symptom of a known cause:** the raise is linked under that issue as a sub-issue (a symptom). No new issue is filed. It closes when the cause merges.
>    - **Root cause found:** the raise and other open issues share a cause nobody filed yet. It raises that cause as a "root cause found" issue, with the symptoms it found linked under it, instead of filing the symptom.
>    - **New:** nothing matches. It looks for similar symptoms first; if none, it files the issue as today.
> 
> The verdict and its evidence show on the issue, so I can see why it was filed, linked or grouped.
> 
> Example: #608 (the fence undoes a worker's clash fix) was a symptom of #475 (the planner starts without main) and should have been linked under it, not filed on its own.
>

**Agent:** I planned [#615](https://github.com/dokima-dev/dokima/issues/615) (checking whether a raised issue shares a known cause) as a split into five stories, not a single plan. Both checks pass. No tests are written yet; each story's own planner writes its tests.

**Why a split:** your ask and two comments add up to 14 separate behaviours. A plan can hold at most 3 per story, so that makes 5 stories.

**The stories:**
1. **The reviewer's starting pack lists every open issue** with its title, summary, links and whether Dokima filed it. This one goes first.
2. **The reviewer records its search and verdict.** A review that files an issue without the list of issues it searched, or without one of the three verdicts, is rejected and says why. The reviewer searches only its pack, never GitHub live. This waits on story 1.
3. **A symptom is linked under its known cause** as a sub-issue, closes when the cause closes as completed, and shows its verdict and evidence. This waits on story 2.
4. **A new root cause is filed as its own issue**, marked "root cause found". The raise and the symptoms it found become its sub-issues, and the issue shows the verdict and evidence. This waits on story 2.
5. **A raise that matches nothing is filed as today**, now showing its verdict, the issues searched and the evidence. This waits on story 2.

**Two questions for you.** The plan goes ahead on my reading of each until you answer:
- **What happens to a symptom?** Your ask says it is "linked under that issue as a sub-issue" and also that "No new issue is filed". Those two can't both hold. I planned that the symptom is filed only as a sub-issue under its cause, never as an issue of its own.
- **Do sub-issues fit here?** Dokima reads an issue with sub-issues as a split. So a cause issue with symptoms under it would never start its planner, and it would close as soon as its symptoms close. That is the reverse of "it closes when the cause merges". The code that does this is `start_waiting` and `close_if_done` in `dokima/agent.py`. I kept sub-issues as you asked, but plain links might fit better.
